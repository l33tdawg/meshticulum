Active goal: 3 useful kB/s at SF5.

The active SF5/BW500 goal is at least 3,000 useful file bytes/s from sender startup through authenticated receipt, with publisher and whole-file verification. Above 2,800 bytes/s remains the net-win checkpoint. Neither has been measured on the current SF5 recovery path. The four-frame estimate of about 2.96 useful kB/s assumes ideal pacing and excludes host work, startup and losses.

New source work adds bounded serial-readiness wakeups, host cipher timings, opt-in native profiling and an explicitly negotiated eight-frame MCU train. The eight-frame path keeps individual durable reservations and releases, unchanged listening floors and legacy four-frame defaults. Its hardware performance is still unqualified. The separate batch prototype remains dormant because its current preparation and USB costs do not justify production integration. Read the implementation, controls and remaining qualification work.

SF5 recovery after alpha.3

A fresh 2,000-byte file on the reviewed SF5/BW500 source completed in 2.554067 seconds including sender startup, or 0.783065 useful kB/s. One selective retry was submitted 16.725 ms after three later receipts identified the missing packet. All 22 physical attempts reconcile, both endpoints closed normally, and tracked services stayed unchanged.

The faster waveform still leaves host and control costs: 752.365 ms passed before the first DATA submission, and 772.067 ms after that had no DATA outstanding. These intervals are not all removable delay. The receiver also took 284.617 ms from the hole-filling packet to its next cumulative ACK under the existing quiet rule. Next work targets those costs and compares equal workloads on SF5 and SF7. This one-part case does not establish sustained 2–3 kB/s, range or a paired profile speedup.

The exact candidate is 5431846; the published alpha.3 assets remain unchanged. Read the source controls, exact wheel identity, retained earlier trials and sanitized physical record.

Alpha.3 / Host accounting and phase timings

Find where the host waits.

Attempt-journal accounting now reads durable aggregate totals instead of scanning every retained attempt on each flow step. Reservation and aggregate updates share the same FULL commit; opening the journal reconstructs totals from the original history. Charged debt, uncertain outcomes and recovery fences are retained.

In one fixed-order macOS comparison with 10,000 synthetic retained attempts, the snapshot median changed from 166.250 to 3.334 microseconds. The improvement concerns local journal work. Small-history reservations can cost more, and no radio rate follows from this result. Inspect the profiles and limits.

The checkout examples accept --profile-host to report 24 bounded phases covering queue reservation, USB write/wait/pacing, physical release and flow steps. Timings are inclusive and overlap. The serial writer also rechecks its original deadline after writable readiness, before another write.

Recorded / 24 October 2026 / LoRa at about 20 metres

Let the MCU queue DATA.
Keep the decisions on the host.

Two Heltec V4 radios about 20 metres apart completed two fresh 8,164-byte signed files B → A, first synchronously and then with the opt-in MCU queue. Both runs used SF7, BW500 kHz, CR4:5, 921.375 MHz and a 0 dBm chip setting. Publisher and whole-file verification passed in both runs.

The queue increased useful throughput by 16.483915% against the synchronous run. Its rate was 22.025263% above the earlier 888.965 B/s distance sample, which used the older firmware and remains a separate historical observation. This pair used the same MCU boots and radio profile, with no reset between modes. One pair in a fixed order does not establish repeatability, sustained capacity, field range or an upstream ranking.

The MCU accepts up to four already encrypted DATA packets, preserves at least 5 ms of receive dwell after successful receive rearm between packets, and reserves at least 100 ms for reverse listening after four packets or an empty train. An active valid header can defer the next send. The host retains keys, authenticated ACKs, receiver credit, retries and peer-progress decisions; ACK and CONTROL transmissions use the synchronous path.

Physical completion and authenticated receipt have separate evidence.

All 46 queued DATA requests retained QUEUED → ISSUED → STARTED → RF_DONE events and exact release acknowledgements. No unknown outcome was introduced. Successful endpoint completion also passed the code-checked empty STATUS gate; a raw final STATUS body was not retained. The queued physical-drain endpoint came 27.456 ms after authenticated receipt, so it is not included in the 7.526076-second receipt boundary.

Retained event intervals are consistent with the receive policy, but do not independently measure when receive mode became active. The queue overlaps host submission with ongoing radio work; summed DATA driver time stayed near 4.99 seconds in both modes. It does not increase the LoRa physical bitrate or extend earlier GFSK, installation or recovery qualification.

An initial deployment failed before RF and the previous images were restored. A later synchronous baseline stopped after 45 acknowledged DATA packets when an under-sized conservative airtime budget blocked the final DATA; its queued run never started. Both failures and their paid costs remain in the record. The successful pair followed the diagnostic and operation-limit fixes.

SF7 is 1.531× faster than SF8 across six matched pairs.

Two complete BW500/CR4/5 campaigns measured median sender-ARM-to-receipt rates of 1.103 kB/s (8.820 kbit/s) at SF7 and 0.720 kB/s (5.759 kbit/s) at SF8. All 12 chunks and authenticated receipts passed. The MCU's 1,024-attempt cap stood at 380 after both campaigns, leaving 644 attempts of headroom, with the same boot generation disarmed and zero receive drops. This is a finite profile comparison, not a range or upstream ranking.

The HTTP gateway now meets a normal 30-second browser timeout. Three consecutive links requests fetched example.com and example.org through unit A's internet connection and returned them over the radio in 17.77–17.99 seconds; gateway completion was 16.81–17.08 seconds. A real lightweight HTTP search and result page also rendered in about 20 seconds. A bounded 3-second startup gap removed the pre-fix 52.8-second race. Google still redirects to HTTPS and its search page exceeds the 16 KiB cap. This is an application gateway, not transparent IP, HTTPS CONNECT or a TUN interface.

Continue verified work.

Isolated imports and all 67 runtime files matched the installed distribution; neither endpoint loaded an editable checkout, SDK zip or system site packages.

Historical baseline / Continuing alpha work

Build on the measured baseline.

Speed matters when the transfer finishes correctly. The 1.0.0-alpha release records useful encrypted delivery above 1 kB/s (8 kbit/s) with sender startup included on the declared near-bench GFSK250 path. The next work extends completion, recovery and hardware coverage while keeping setup, verification and cleanup time visible.

  • Performance

    Repeat the LoRa queue comparison.

    Repeat and vary the order of synchronous and queued runs, then test larger files and loss. Keep the measured 16.48% gain scoped to the first pair. Report useful decoded bytes, startup, ACK and retry cost, completion and drain within the original deadlines, alongside the earlier GFSK results.

  • Reliability

    Finish safely when a link fails.

    Exercise loss, final-ACK recovery, expiry, replay refusal and interruption. Preserve verified work, original peer and boot bindings, and attempt debt. Prove disarm and ownership closure; an uncertain write keeps its reservation.

  • Deployment

    Extend the published setup path.

    The clean installed-wheel check demonstrates full-file completion and continuation after a verified piece on the declared GFSK250 path. The published alpha supplies the SDK and a source-build path with app-only backup and rollback instructions and required notices. Broader hardware and in-flight recovery remain separate work; optional prebuilt firmware needs its own source, object, relinking and notice review.

  • Qualification

    Keep each advertised path within its evidence.

    Declare the supported radio path and its setup, timing and recovery limits. Wider mixed-path failure/resume and native private owner, consent, replay and forward-secrecy qualification remain programme work. The consuming application must qualify its integration; a public SDK alpha does not complete that work.

Later / Beyond the alpha baseline

Measure the next step.

Optional stretch

4 to 5 kB/s useful delivery.

Pursue faster profiles only with verified radio and firmware support. Measure completion, range, retries and power alongside throughput. This stretch target is separate from the alpha floor and has no promised date.

Feasibility work

Post-quantum and hybrid protocols.

Study handshake size, fragmentation, memory, processing cost and key distribution before choosing a deployment path. The current default protocols use classical cryptography. A prototype or a passing source test is not a security qualification.

Read the post-quantum work

Published MeshCore and Reticulum figures help define questions worth measuring. Their hardware, workload, encryption and timing conditions belong beside any comparison. A faster number on one path does not establish a general speed or security ranking.

One family / Separate projects

A transport others can build on.

Meshticulum is the reusable protocol, cryptography, reliable flow, drivers and verified-resource SDK. SAGE Lantern owns its product, STL and printing files, private SAGE adapters, device policy and deployment. Lantern's launch is separate; the dependency runs from Lantern to Meshticulum.
